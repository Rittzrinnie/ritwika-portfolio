01 / The shift

Software this complex had never been a simple checkout.

Cadence products have specialized capabilities, licensing and compatibility considerations. Cadence Shop introduced an independent path for customers to explore products and begin trials or purchases online, without making the technical detail disappear.

My work focused on understanding these journeys, shaping the interface and validating patterns across releases. Cuboids provided the foundation for scalable, accessible UI.

CONSTRAINT 01Specialist products

Buyers needed accurate technical information while navigating a large product range.

CONSTRAINT 02Different levels of expertise

Experienced engineers and newer customers arrived with very different levels of familiarity.

CONSTRAINT 03Phased delivery

The interface had to evolve without rebuilding common patterns for each release.

The design principle
Show the right information when it becomes useful — not all of it at once.

This principle connected the catalogue, user onboarding and accessibility decisions.

02 / Decisions in practice

Three problems. Three connected design responses.

Each decision below links an observed or synthesized constraint to an interaction pattern and a piece of original project evidence.

01 — DISCOVERY

Help people find the right software.

Technical product terminology could make a catalogue difficult to navigate, especially for users who knew their goal but not the exact product name.

The response was to organize entry points around intent and workflow, then use categories and filters before exposing deeper specifications.

DecisionGoal → workflow category → product set → trial or purchase. The benefit is a clearer starting point, not a claim that every user completed their task faster.
02 — ACCOUNT ACCESS

Remove uncertainty before the trial.

Self-service depends on the quality of sign-in, registration and password recovery. Unclear requirements and generic validation create avoidable hesitation.

Design changes documented in the Baymard review included clearer Cadence ID benefits, contextual tooltips and password rules that update as users type.

DecisionMake form requirements understandable at the moment of action and distinguish error recovery from successful completion.
ORIGINAL FIGMA / RECOVERY STATE

Make password recovery understandable.

Account recovery was treated as part of the broader self-service journey: users needed explicit instructions and clear next steps rather than opaque errors.

These screens are direct exports from the original Figma file, not recreations.

03 — ACCESSIBILITY

Design for people who navigate differently.

Accessibility was addressed through multiple releases instead of a single final pass. The existing work includes contrast, visible focus, form labels and error messaging.

Using Cuboids for common patterns helped make fixes repeatable as the store expanded.

DecisionTreat keyboard and form interaction as part of core delivery, not post-launch decoration.
03 / Validation

Use an audit to decide what to fix — and what to hand off.

The case study documents two Baymard guideline reviews from March 2022: one for the existing staging shop and one for the new design. The value is in comparing concrete guidelines and tracking the status of design and engineering responses.

DOCUMENTED DESIGN FIXESClearer form guidance

Cadence ID benefits, live password rules, contextual tooltips and more specific actions.

ENGINEERING DEPENDENCIESNot every item was a design-only fix

Country autocomplete, location targeting and several validation behaviors required implementation work.

FOLLOW-UP PRIORITIESUnresolved items remained

Captcha replacement, phone-input masks and password-reset email feedback were listed as further fixes.

04 / Design system

More than one new storefront.

The solution depended on repeatable design decisions: form states, accessible controls and consistent patterns built with the Cuboids design language.

Design documentation lived alongside requirements, research, testing and governance records. These are part of the delivery story, but the main case study should keep the screens and decisions in focus.

05 / Outcome

What the evidence supports.

The strongest documented outcome in this case study is a coherent self-service design direction and a set of concrete audit-led improvements across discovery, account access and accessibility.

Broader claims such as faster discovery, increased confidence or reduced drop-off should be published with their measurement source and period. This draft intentionally does not turn those expectations into unsupported metrics.

  • Shipped scope: 0→1 storefront design work across a phased release process.
  • Validated design activity: Baymard comparisons, research synthesis and staging testing.
  • Cross-functional constraint: several behaviors depended on engineering implementation.

Reflection: Making specialist software available online meant sequencing complexity—helping people move from a goal to a confident next action while retaining the necessary technical information.
