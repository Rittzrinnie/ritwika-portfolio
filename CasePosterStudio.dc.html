PROJECT OVERVIEW

The Volkswagen R-Postergenerator invites visitors to create personalized artwork featuring their favorite R models. Users can choose a vehicle, explore paint colors and perspectives, personalize the background, and download their artwork for personal use.

My contribution focused on connecting these interactions into a coherent experience. I worked on the UX decisions and UI design that would help visitors understand the concept, begin creating, and move toward a finished artwork.

THE CENTRAL DESIGN QUESTION

How could I guide visitors with different intentions from discovery to a finished artwork, within the Volkswagen R brand?

THE CHALLENGE

The experience needed to serve visitors with different levels of intent. Some would arrive ready to create; others would need inspiration or an explanation first. Returning visitors needed a way to revisit an existing artwork.

I approached the website as a connected journey, with each section answering a specific question:

01
What can I create?
02
How does it work?
03
What could my design look like?
04
How do I start?
05
How do I return to something I already made?

This helped frame the landing page as part of the product experience.

UX DECISIONS
UX DECISION 1

Show the outcome before asking for effort

The hero presents artwork in the context of screens and personal devices, accompanied by a prominent creation button.

My rationale was to make the value of participation immediately understandable. Showing the finished output gives visitors a concrete reason to explore the tool.

I prioritized a clear primary action so that visitors who were already interested could move directly into creation.

UX DECISION 2

Explain the experience in three simple stages

The landing page introduces the journey through three stages: choose an R model, personalize the artwork, and download it.

I used this structure to establish expectations before visitors entered the editor. The explanation communicates the overall task without requiring users to understand every customization option upfront.

These are the three conceptual stages of the experience; the editor can break them into smaller interactions where necessary.

UX DECISION 3

Support inspiration and action on the same page

The artwork gallery gives visitors examples of what they can create. A further creation call to action provides an entry point after browsing.

My rationale was to support visitors who need to see possibilities before making their own choices. The gallery makes an abstract promise tangible and helps users form an initial creative direction.

Placing another entry point after the examples allows visitors to act when they feel ready.

UX DECISION 4

Give returning users a direct route back

The landing page includes an artwork-code field that allows users to revisit and change previously created images.

I treated returning visitors as a distinct user journey. Their immediate goal is to recover an existing design, so the page provides a recognizable route into that task.

This extends the experience beyond a single creation session and supports continued experimentation.

UX DECISION 5

Keep supporting information available

The FAQ section addresses practical questions about the generator, customization, available models, and additional software.

My rationale was to keep the main explanation concise while making further detail easy to find. Expandable questions let visitors explore relevant information without requiring everyone to read it.

DESIGN THINKING → TRADE-OFFS

The decisions behind the experience

These are the design considerations that shaped the solution. They explain intent and trade-offs, rather than claiming outcomes that were not independently measured.

01 · Inspiration versus speed
Give curious visitors a reason to explore while keeping a direct creation path visible for goal-driven visitors.

Design implication: Freedom versus guidance

02 · Sequence vehicle, viewpoint, color and artwork choices to lower the initial decision burden without removing customization.
Brand expression versus usability

Design implication: Use Volkswagen R's performance-led visual identity while preserving clear controls, progress feedback and an obvious final download action.

UI DESIGN DIRECTION

The visual direction connects the creative experience to Volkswagen R through blue tones, dark backgrounds, prominent vehicle imagery, and clear typography.

I organized the interface around three priorities:

Make the artwork the focal point.

Vehicle imagery and finished compositions communicate the experience more effectively than lengthy descriptions.

Make actions easy to recognize.

Prominent, rounded buttons establish a consistent visual treatment for starting the creation journey.

Balance expressive imagery with readable structure.

The campaign visuals create atmosphere, while headings, short explanations, and spacing help visitors understand the next step.

The intention was to carry the energy of the brand into an interface that remains easy to navigate.

END-TO-END USER WORKFLOW
Discover→Understand→Create→Personalize→Download→Revisit
  1. 01
    Discover the experience.

    The visitor encounters the hero and sees examples of the possible output.

  2. 02
    Understand the process.

    A short introduction explains model selection, personalization, and download.

  3. 03
    Enter the generator.

    The visitor selects the creation action and encounters the usage notice.

  4. 04
    Choose the vehicle.

    The advertised journey includes selecting an R model, paint color, and perspective.

  5. 05
    Personalize the artwork.

    Background and color choices let the visitor shape the composition.

  6. 06
    Download the result.

    The artwork can be used as a personal wallpaper or shared socially, subject to the stated usage conditions.

  7. 07
    Return to edit.

    An artwork code provides a route back to an existing design.

The gallery and FAQs support this journey by providing inspiration and answering questions before users begin.

OUTCOME

The resulting experience connects brand discovery with personal creation. Visitors can understand the concept, explore inspiration, create an artwork, and return to a previous design.

My contribution brought UX structure and visual design together across that journey, with a focus on clear entry points, understandable choices, and a consistent brand experience.

REFLECTION

The central design lesson was that a creative experience starts before the editor opens. The landing page establishes motivation, examples help users imagine a result, and a clear explanation makes the first step feel manageable.

For me, the project was about designing the connections between these moments so that the overall experience felt coherent from discovery through creation and return.
